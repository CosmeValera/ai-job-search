# Italy: *regime impatriati* (inbound worker tax regime)

## Read this first

**Nothing in this file has been verified by a qualified source.** It is a transcription of
notes the candidate produced in conversation with an AI chatbot on 2026-08-16. No Italian
accountant (*commercialista*), tax lawyer, or Agenzia delle Entrate ruling has been consulted,
and the candidate has not read the underlying legislation.

This matters more than usual because the whole thing is **self-declared**. There is no upfront
approval step that would catch an error: the candidate signs a declaration, the employer applies
the benefit in payroll, and any mistake surfaces years later as a repayment demand with interest.

**How to use this file when evaluating an offer:**

- Treat every figure below as an illustration of *scale*, never as an input to a decision.
- **Never quote a net-with-regime figure to an employer, in a negotiation, or in any document.**
- When a Milan offer becomes real, the regime moves from "interesting" to "must be verified before
  signing" - see Gate 1 below.
- Never let this regime justify accepting a Milan gross below the 45K anchor in
  `04-job-evaluation.md`. The gross is what survives if the regime does not apply, what the next
  employer anchors on, and what pension contributions are calculated from.

## What the candidate's notes say

### Mechanism
Only 50% of Italian employment income is taxed, for 5 years. Cap €600K/year. 60% exemption
instead of 50% if a minor child lives in Italy during the period.

Legal basis cited: art. 5, D.Lgs. 209/2023, as amended by L. 132/2025. Applies to moves from
1 Jan 2024 onward.

### Eligibility as the candidate assessed it

| Requirement | Candidate's status |
|---|---|
| Not tax-resident in Italy for the 3 preceding years | Satisfied automatically - never lived in Italy |
| High qualification / specialization | Claimed via the ICT-experience route (below) |
| Work performed mainly in Italy | Satisfied by taking an Italian job |
| Commit to 4 years of Italian tax residence | **The real commitment** |
| Not the same employer group as the current one | Flagged - see the correction below |

### The qualification route

The FP Grado Superior qualifications (DAM + DAW) **do not** qualify on the study route: that route
needs a post-secondary qualification of at least 3 years, or EQF level 6. Técnico Superior is
2 years and EQF level 5.

The route claimed instead is professional experience: art. 27-quater TUI letter (d) allows at least
3 years of relevant professional experience in the previous 7 for ICT specialists in ISCO-08 groups
133 and 25. Software developer falls in group 25. The candidate has roughly 4 years total
experience and, as of September 2026, roughly 3 years at GMV alone.

The notes cite interpelli 71/E and 74/E of 2025 as confirming that this route is applicable to
the impatriati regime.

### Illustrative value (Milan, single, no children, candidate's 2026 assumptions)

| Gross (RAL) | Ordinary net | Net with regime | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| €40K | ~€27.5K | ~€34.6K | ~€7.1K/yr |
| €50K | ~€32.6K | ~€42.1K | ~€9.5K/yr |

Comparison figures from the same notes: €50K in Madrid nets ~€36.1K, €50K in Berlin nets ~€32.3K.
Rates used were IRPEF 2026 (23/33/43%), INPS 9.19%, plus Lombardia and Milano *addizionali*.

### Applying
No application to the tax agency and no prior approval. The candidate hands the Italian employer a
*dichiarazione sostitutiva di atto notorio* asserting the requirements are met, under their own
responsibility, and the employer applies it in payroll. **The burden is evidence retention against a
later audit, not paperwork upfront.**

## Corrections and cautions to apply on top of the notes

These are flags raised against the notes above. They are **also unverified** - they mark places to
push the *commercialista* harder, not settled answers.

**1. The "not the same employer group" line is probably too strict, and it may be costing an option.**
The reformed regime does not appear to bar an intra-group move outright. The mechanism recalled is
an *extended lookback*: where the worker will work in Italy for the same employer they worked for
abroad, or another company in that group, the minimum period of **non-residence in Italy** stretches
from 3 years to roughly 6 or 7. Since the candidate has never been resident in Italy at all, an
extended lookback of any length is satisfied trivially. If that reading holds, **a move to GMV's
Italian entity would still qualify** - and an internal transfer is one of the cheaper routes to Milan,
so it should not be ruled out on this basis without checking. Verify before discarding the option.

**2. The net figures look optimistic by roughly 5%.** A rough independent pass on the €50K row -
INPS deducted first, 50% of the remainder exempt, IRPEF plus Lombardia/Milano *addizionali* on
what is left - lands nearer €39-40K net than €42.1K. Directionally the notes are right and the gain
is real; the exact number is not reliable. Do not carry €42.1K into any comparison.

**3. The 4-year lock-in is the dominant risk and it interacts badly with why Milan is wanted.**
Leaving Italy before completing 4 years of tax residence means repaying the entire benefit with
interest - on a €50K salary, plausibly €38K or more at stake. The reason Milan ranks first is a
personal tie to one specific person. If that tie changes, the regime converts a personal decision
into a five-figure financial penalty for leaving. **The regime makes a good Milan offer better; it
also makes a bad Milan situation much harder to exit.** Weigh it as both.

**4. The regime does not start on the day of the move.** Italian tax residence needs more than 183
days in a calendar year. A move in the second half of a year generally means that year does not
count, so the benefit and the 4-year clock both start the following January - with the intervening
months taxed without relief. As of **2026-08-16 this year is already past that point**, so a 2026
move means the clock starts January 2027 and runs to the end of 2030. An H1 move is materially
better than an H2 one; if an offer lands late in a year, the start date is worth negotiating.

**5. The citations are unconfirmed.** D.Lgs. 209/2023 as the source of the 2024 reform, the 50%
exemption, the €600K cap and the 4-year residence commitment are consistent with what is generally
understood about the reform. "L. 132/2025" and "interpelli 71/E and 74/E of 2025" could not be
confirmed here and must be checked against a current source before being relied on. The ICT-experience
route via the Blue Card qualification framework is plausible but is exactly the kind of detail an
audit would test.

## Two gates

### Gate 0: do this while still employed at GMV

Evidence for the ICT-experience route has to come from the current and previous employers, and it
gets harder to obtain after leaving. Collect now, independently of whether any Milan offer ever
appears - the cost is low and the option value is high.

- [ ] *Certificado de empresa* or reference letter stating job title **and actual duties**, worded so
      the work reads unambiguously as software development (must map to ISCO-08 group 25). A letter
      that says only "Frontend Developer" with no duties is weaker evidence than one describing the
      development work performed.
- [ ] Employment contract, plus any role change or promotion documents
- [ ] *Vida laboral*
- [ ] Payslips
- [ ] The same set for the pre-GMV roles (Capgemini, Seanchas) as a buffer

### Gate 1: before signing any Italian offer

- [ ] One paid session with an Italian *commercialista*. Bring: the ISCO-08 framing of the GMV role,
      the FP-does-not-qualify / experience-route-does argument, and the Spain-exit / Italy-entry
      residency split.
- [ ] Confirm whether the employer is in GMV's group and, if so, whether correction 1 above holds
- [ ] Confirm the contractual role description matches the development experience being claimed
- [ ] Model the start date against the 183-day rule and negotiate it if the year is late
- [ ] Recompute net with the *commercialista*'s numbers, not the ones in this file
- [ ] Decide explicitly whether the 4-year commitment is acceptable **on its own terms**, assuming the
      personal reason for Milan does not hold for all four years
