# Interview Tips

Lessons earned from real interviews, not generic advice. Every entry traces back to a specific
postmortem in `postmortems/`.

Read the checklist below the morning of every interview. Add to the lessons only when something
actually happened that proves the point.

---

## Pre-interview checklist

Do all of this the day before, not the hour before.

- [ ] **Write three answers out, in full.** *Why this company* / *what culture do you want* /
      *why are you leaving GMV*. Written, not "I know roughly what I'd say."
- [ ] **Read the company's culture page, values page or handbook.** Culture questions are open-book.
      Cross-check it against what you actually want so the overlap is real, not performed.
- [ ] **Read the job posting again the night before.** The answers to "what culture do you expect"
      and "why us" are often printed on it.
- [ ] **Rehearse aloud, in English.** Not in your head. Record 90 seconds and play it back — C1
      goes rusty without use, and the first two minutes of an interview are the worst time to warm up.
- [ ] **Prepare 5 questions for them.** Specific to the company, not reusable boilerplate.
      At least one should force them to state something concrete (see Lesson 5).
- [ ] **Know your number and your rule.** If a band is published: *"the range you posted works for
      me — given the scope I'd be looking at the upper half."* Never name a figure first. The current
      37.5K never enters the conversation, at any stage, in any form.
      Full rules: `.claude/skills/job-application-assistant/04-job-evaluation.md`.
- [ ] **Ask about the process** in the screening call: how many stages, is there a take-home,
      what's the timeline.

## Lessons

### 1. Culture questions are open-book — never improvise them
"What culture do you expect from us?" is not a trick and not a values test. It is checking whether
you have thought about what environment makes you good. Guessing what they want to hear is both the
obvious failure mode and easy to detect. Companies of any size publish this: a culture playbook, a
values page, or the bullets at the bottom of the posting.

**Do:** answer from true preferences, then let them overlap with what the company published.
Cosme's real, usable ones — small team where you see the whole product; code review as a real
conversation, not a rubber stamp; direct access to the people using the software; knowing *why*
something is being built, not just *what*.

*Source: [koppla, 2026-09-01](postmortems/2026-09-01-koppla.md)*

### 2. "Why this company" must be about the company
"My brothers live in Berlin" answers *why Berlin*. "The salary is better" answers *why leave*.
Neither answers *why you*. The city and the money are true and fine to mention — second, briefly,
after a company-specific reason has already landed.

*Source: [koppla, 2026-09-01](postmortems/2026-09-01-koppla.md)*

### 3. Specificity beats enthusiasm
You do not have to love the company. The bar is one concrete thing that proves you read about them —
a product decision, their stage, who their customers are, a technical constraint of their domain.
Any specific observation outperforms any amount of performed passion.

There is also a genuine thread available every time: **operational and infrastructure software over
consumer and ad-tech.** Galileo at GMV, ADIF rail at Capgemini — software that coordinates physical,
expensive things where a delay costs real money. That preference is true, it is unusual, and most
candidates cannot claim it. Use it.

*Source: [koppla, 2026-09-01](postmortems/2026-09-01-koppla.md)*

### 4. Rehearsing in your head is not rehearsing
A prepared argument can come out inverted under pressure. At koppla the point was that GMV/Capgemini
and koppla are the **same** category — physical-world software. It came out as the opposite, and the
interviewer noticed the answer did not match her own product. Saying it out loud once would have
caught it.

*Source: [koppla, 2026-09-01](postmortems/2026-09-01-koppla.md)*

### 5. Make them state the success criteria out loud
The strongest question found so far: *"In six months from now, what would tell you that having hired me worked?"*
It is hard to answer with boilerplate, it tells you what the job actually is, and it reads as someone
thinking about delivering rather than about getting in.

### 6. Verify a startup's self-description
koppla's posting said *"we are not looking for hypergrowth."* The screener said headcount went from
~20 to ~44 in one year with another raise in progress. Postings describe the culture a company wants
to have. Ask for the headcount number in the call.

*Source: [koppla, 2026-09-01](postmortems/2026-09-01-koppla.md)*

### 7. Funded startups run long processes — budget for it
koppla: four stages — HR screening, coding challenge, team interview, CEO interview. Assume a
take-home at any funded company and ask about it up front so it does not arrive as a surprise
alongside three other pipelines.

*Source: [koppla, 2026-09-01](postmortems/2026-09-01-koppla.md)*
